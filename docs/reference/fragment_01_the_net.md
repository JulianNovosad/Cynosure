# Fragment 1: The Net / Cyberspace

The Net (or Cyberspace) is the vast telecommunications network of the Cyberpunk universe—analogous to the early real-world internet, but far more extensive, linking appliances, cybernetics, and computers via hard lines, radio, cell networks, and microwave transmitters.

Access requires a modem. Casual users interact through terminals and keyboards, but professionals jack in through cybermodems (cyberdecks), experiencing the Net in 3D via direct neural interface. Speed is everything; keyboard jockeys are left in the dust.

The pre-DataKrash network is called the Old NET; the post-Blackwall network is the Shallow Net, mostly NET Architectures and Data Pools.

**Icons** are 3D avatars representing people, programs, and systems. Complexity scales with bandwidth and memory—most of the Net looks like *Tron*, but high-end rigs can render photorealistic *Matrix*-grade environments. The default interface is now universal and Tron-like.

**Ihara-Grubb Transformation Algorithms** make Netspace analogous to realspace—distances, elevations, and movement feel intuitive. Connection quality alters the terrain: instability renders as mountains, smooth links as grid lines.

**Data Fortresses** are 3D representations of computer systems. Their appearance depends on resources, ranging from simple Tron-like structures to photorealistic castles or space stations. Sysops control the environment. **Data walls** represent system security; **code gates** are the normal entry points. Inside, files might appear as cabinets in locked offices—intuitive spatial metaphors for memory and data.
